\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Pruning and quantization are routinely stacked to compress language models,
and compression rates that ignore the storage of the sparsity mask make the
combination look better than it is. Inside one Hessian-compensated sweep, we
compare joint, prune-then-quantize and quantize-then-prune compression of
SmolLM2 and Qwen2.5 models from 135M to \largestmodel{} parameters. With the
mask counted, 25\% pruning with 4-bit weights costs as much as plain 4-bit
quantization and only ties it, while 50\% pruning
with 4-bit weights beats plain 3-bit quantization at 3.0 bits per weight on
\resFrontierThreeWins{} of \resFrontierThreeModels{} models; below 3 bits,
plain quantization collapses. Quantizing first is the worst order in most
settings, also where a weaker-first rule predicts it to be best, and neither a
stale quantization grid nor a mask chosen on rounded weights explains why. We evaluate with KL
divergence, answer flips and full benchmark splits.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Pruning and quantization both have accurate one-shot post-training versions
for language models, SparseGPT~\cite{frantar2023sparsegpt} and
GPTQ~\cite{frantar2023gptq}, cast in one optimal-brain-surgeon (OBS)
framework~\cite{frantar2022obc}, and they are routinely stacked. In earlier
work~\cite{laborde2025semantic} we compared stacked configurations at equal
theoretical compression rate (TCr), which counts only the stored values. An
unstructured sparse matrix must also store where its non-zeros are: with a
1-bit mask, 25\% pruning with 4-bit weights costs 4.0 bits per weight, as
much as plain 4-bit, not 3.0. SparseGPT stores such a bitmask and reports 50\%
sparsity with 4-bit weights to be more accurate than 3-bit quantization at the
same memory, for OPT models from 2.7B parameters
up~\cite{frantar2023sparsegpt}; on small models, mostly of 1B to 3.8B
parameters, quantization preserves quality better than
pruning~\cite{zhou2025revisiting}.

The order of the two steps is open too. Harma et
al.~\cite{harma2025interplay} prove that sparsity and max-scaled quantization
are non-orthogonal and that sparsity should come first, for magnitude pruning
without compensation; for SparseGPT and Wanda they report a much smaller
order effect, measured with 6- to 8-bit formats. Kim et
al.~\cite{kim2026order} propose to apply the weaker perturbation first, in
sequential pipelines that also quantize activations and the KV cache. Bouriel
et al.~\cite{bouriel2026interaction} combine magnitude pruning and
bitsandbytes quantization on models of about 1B parameters; for 4-bit hybrids
at 20\% sparsity the order changes the interaction by at most 1--2 points.
None of these uses a joint sweep, and each uses magnitude pruning or
quantizes activations. We ask the question inside the one-shot,
Hessian-compensated, weight-only pipeline used in
practice~\cite{frantar2023sparsegpt,guo2026obr}, on
SmolLM2~\cite{benallal2025smollm2} and Qwen2.5~\cite{qwen2024qwen25} models
compressed by one sweep in which only the order differs, and count storage as
SparseGPT does. We contribute a storage frontier with the mask counted, an
exact free-zero effect, a same-Hessian test of a layer-error decomposition, a
test of two order rules, a negative result on the cause of the order effect,
and an evaluation with KL divergence and answer flips~\cite{dutta2024accuracy}.

\section{One sweep, three orders}
\label{sec:framework}

For a linear layer with weights $W$ and calibration inputs $X$, post-training
compression minimizes $\|WX-\widehat W X\|_F^2$ under a sparsity or precision
constraint; with $H=2XX^\top$ and $\Delta=W-\widehat W$ the objective is
$\mathrm{tr}(\Delta H\Delta^\top)$, and we report the normalized layer error
$E=\mathrm{tr}(\Delta H\Delta^\top)/\mathrm{tr}(WHW^\top)$. OBS-type methods
compress the columns in order, compensating each column's error on the
remaining ones through $H^{-1}$. SparseGPT zeroes, per block of 128
columns, the weights of smallest saliency $w^2/[H^{-1}]_{jj}^2$; GPTQ rounds
to a min--max asymmetric grid fitted per row and group of 128 columns. One
sweep implements three combinations.
\emph{Joint}~\cite{frantar2022obc,frantar2023sparsegpt}: in each block the
mask is selected first, the grid is fitted on the kept weights, and every
column is pruned and quantized before compensation. \emph{P$\to$Q}: a pruning
sweep returns $W_P$ and its mask $M$; a quantization sweep keeps $M$ fixed, so
pruned weights stay zero while their error is still compensated.
\emph{Q$\to$P}: a quantization sweep returns $W_Q$ and its grid $G$; a pruning
sweep selects the mask and projects every compensated weight back onto $G$.
Joint and P$\to$Q are \emph{mask-first}, Q$\to$P is \emph{grid-first}. Layers
are compressed sequentially, each calibrated on the outputs of the already
compressed ones.

\section{Counting storage and interaction}
\label{sec:accounting}

\textbf{Storage.} For sparsity $s$ and bit-width $b$,
$\mathrm{TCr}=1-\frac{b}{16}(1-s)$~\cite{laborde2025semantic} counts
${b(1-s)}$ bits per weight, the stored values only. With one mask bit per
weight, as SparseGPT's comparison implies~\cite{frantar2023sparsegpt}, a layer
costs $B(s,b)={b(1-s)}+\mathbf{1}_{s>0}+{(16+b)/128}$ bits per weight; the last
term, a 16-bit scale and a $b$-bit zero point per group of 128 weights
(\resGroupOverheadBFour{} bit at $b=4$, \resGroupOverheadBThree{} at $b=3$),
differs by at most $1/128$ bit between the configurations we compare, so we
leave it out. The binary entropy $H_2(s)$ is the cost of an ideally coded
i.i.d.\ mask (1 bit at $s=50\%$, 0.81 at $25\%$), and
entropy coding of the stored symbols, zeros included (symbol entropy), would
also make the zeros of plain rounding free. The three orders of a cell
$(s,b)$ cost the same; across cells we compare at equal $B$: 25\%+4-bit with
plain 4-bit at 4.0 bits, 50\%+4-bit with plain 3-bit at 3.0 bits.

\textbf{Interaction.} With layer errors $E_P$, $E_Q$ and $E_{PQ}$ of pruning
only, quantization only and a combination, $I_E=E_{PQ}-(E_P+E_Q)$ is negative
when the combination is sub-additive. Write
$\langle A,B\rangle_H=\mathrm{tr}(AHB^\top)$ and
$\|A\|_H^2=\langle A,A\rangle_H$, and take a mask $M$ and a fixed grid
$q(\cdot)$ without compensation. With $P=W\odot M$ the pruned weights and
$R=W-q(W)=R_M+R_{\bar M}$ the rounding residual on pruned and kept entries,
$\Delta_P=P$, $\Delta_Q=R$, $\Delta_{PQ}=P+R_{\bar M}$ and, up to the common
normalization,
\[
  I_E \;\propto\; \underbrace{-\,\|R_M\|_H^2}_{\text{absorption}\;\le\;0}
  \;+\; \underbrace{2\langle P,R_{\bar M}\rangle_H
  -2\langle R_M,R_{\bar M}\rangle_H}_{\text{cross terms}}.
\]
Rounding errors on pruned weights disappear, and the cross terms vanish for a
diagonal $H$, since $P$, $R_M$ and $R_{\bar M}$ have disjoint supports within
each row. The guarantee $E_{PQ}\le E_P+E_Q$ thus needs no compensation, a
fixed grid and a diagonal $H$. Language-model inputs are correlated, so the
cross terms can take either sign, and in sequential runs $E_P$, $E_Q$ and
$E_{PQ}$ come from different Hessians from the second block on; we therefore
test sub-additivity with one shared Hessian per layer.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We compress all linear layers of the decoder blocks of SmolLM2-135M and -360M
and Qwen2.5-0.5B\ifEsix{} and SmolLM2-1.7B\fi. Calibration uses 128 random
windows of 2048 tokens from the first validation shard of
C4~\cite{raffel2020t5}, a damping of $0.01\,\overline{\mathrm{diag}(H)}$, and
blocks and groups of 128 columns. The grid (E1) crosses $s\in\{25,33.3,50\}\%$
with $b\in\{4,3\}$ in the three orders, plus each method alone ($b=8$
included)\ifEfour; E4 adds 2-bit, 60\% and 60\%+4-bit\fi. E2 recomputes every
variant of a layer with one dense-model Hessian, for every second
block\ifEthree; E3 repeats its arms as full runs\fi. All runs use fp32 on
NVIDIA L4 GPUs, TF32 off. We report WikiText-2~\cite{merity2017pointer} and
C4 perplexity, the token-level KL divergence from the dense to the compressed
model on WikiText-2, and zero-shot accuracy with
lm-eval~\cite{gao2026lmeval} on the full splits of nine tasks, logged per item
(ARC-Easy and -Challenge, HellaSwag, PIQA, WinoGrande, OpenBookQA, BoolQ,
SciQ, LAMBADA). Flips are the items whose correctness differs from the dense
model, pooled over the tasks~\cite{dutta2024accuracy}; intervals are paired
bootstraps (\resBootB{} resamples), and orders are compared item by item with
exact McNemar tests. Code and results:
\url{github.com/martin-cousseau/esann2027-compose}; models:
\url{huggingface.co/martincousseau/esann2027-compose-v2}; runs:
\url{wandb.ai/martin-css/esann2027-compose-v2}.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\textbf{Storage: pruning pays only when it replaces a bit.}
Table~\ref{tab:storage} and Fig.~\ref{fig:frontier} compare configurations
on the storage axis. At 4.0 bits
per weight, 25\% pruning brings nothing over plain 4-bit: on SmolLM2-135M its
best order, P$\to$Q, reaches a perplexity of
\resPplSmolSmallSTwentyFiveBFourPQ{} against \resPplSmolSmallQuantBFour, and on
every model the best order is within \resFrontierFourGapMaxPct\% of 4-bit. At
3.0 bits, 50\%+4-bit beats plain 3-bit: on SmolLM2-135M, P$\to$Q reaches
\resPplSmolSmallSFiftyBFourPQ{} against \resPplSmolSmallQuantBThree, with
lower KL (\resKlSmolSmallSFiftyBFourPQ{} against \resKlSmolSmallQuantBThree)
and fewer flips (\resFlipsPctSmolSmallSFiftyBFourPQ\% against
\resFlipsPctSmolSmallQuantBThree\%), and its perplexity is lower on
\resFrontierThreeWins{} of \resFrontierThreeModels{} models, by
\resFrontierThreeGainMinPct--\resFrontierThreeGainMaxPct\%; on Qwen2.5-0.5B
the two tie (\resPplQwenSFiftyBFourJoint{} against \resPplQwenQuantBThree). TCr would put
25\%+4-bit at 3.0 bits, where it seems to beat 3-bit by far, as
in~\cite{laborde2025semantic}. An entropy-coded mask leaves the 3.0-bit
comparison unchanged ($H_2(0.5)=1$) and makes 25\%+4-bit 0.19 bit cheaper
than 4-bit at about equal quality. Under symbol entropy, which also frees the
zeros of plain rounding, 3-bit costs \resBitsSymSmolSmallQuantBThree{} bits
per weight on SmolLM2-135M and 50\%+4-bit \resBitsSymSmolSmallSFiftyBFourPQ:
that comparison is no longer at equal storage.

\textbf{Free zeros.} Rounding creates zeros that TCr ignores: 3-bit
quantization sets \resFreeZerosPctSmolSmallQuantBThree\% of the weights of
SmolLM2-135M exactly to zero (4-bit: \resFreeZerosPctSmolSmallQuantBFour\%).
A grid-first 25\% mask then picks only weights that are already zero: Q$\to$P
at 25\%+3-bit is the 3-bit model, with the same perplexity and layer errors,
on \resIdentityQPThreeCount{} of \resIdentityQPThreeModels{} models. TCr
credits it with 2.25 bits per weight and the bitmap with 3.25; it is a
3.0-bit model with free sparsity.

\textbf{Sub-additivity with one Hessian.} With one shared Hessian per
layer (E2), mask-first orders are sub-additive in aggregate in
\resETwoMaskFirstSub{} of \resETwoMaskFirstTotal{} combinations of model, cell
and order. Per layer, the joint sweep
is sub-additive in \resETwoSubJointMinPct--\resETwoSubJointMaxPct\% of the
layers of a cell, P$\to$Q only in \resETwoSubPQMinPct--\resETwoSubPQMaxPct\%.
Its interaction grows with input correlation, as the cross terms allow: the
per-layer ratio has a Spearman correlation of \resETwoRhoMin{} to
\resETwoRhoMax{} ($p\le\resETwoRhoPMax$) with the mean absolute correlation
between the layer's inputs. In block~0, P$\to$Q is super-additive in
\resETwoBlockZeroSuperPQSmolMedium, \resETwoBlockZeroSuperPQSmolSmall{} and
\resETwoBlockZeroSuperPQQwen{} of the six cells on SmolLM2-360M, -135M and
Qwen2.5-0.5B.

\textbf{Order: it matters, but no proposed rule or mechanism explains it.}
Joint has the lowest WikiText-2 perplexity in \resQTwoBestJointPpl{} of
\resQTwoCellsPpl{} cells, P$\to$Q in \resQTwoBestPQPpl{}, Q$\to$P in
\resQTwoBestQPPpl; Q$\to$P is the worst in \resQTwoQPWorstPplBFour{} of
\resQTwoCellsPplBFour{} 4-bit cells and reaches \resQTwoMaxRatio{} times the
best (\resQTwoMaxRatioWhere). Kim et al.'s weaker-first
rule~\cite{kim2026order}, with their compression-equivalent ratio, picks the
better sequential order in \resESevenKimCorrectPplAll{} of
\resESevenKimCellsPplAll{} cells, but in \resESevenKimPredQPCorrectPplAll{} of
the \resESevenKimPredQPPplAll{} where pruning is the stronger step and it
predicts quantizing first. Sparsity first wins in
\resESevenHarmaPQBetterPplAll{} of \resESevenHarmaOrderCellsPplAll, as Harma et
al.\ prove for magnitude pruning~\cite{harma2025interplay}, yet the reverse
order costs up to \resESevenHarmaMaxGapPplAllPct\% in perplexity. The quantize-first
penalty has two proposed causes: a stale grid, fitted before pruning (our earlier reading), or a mask
chosen on rounded weights (as the analysis of~\cite{harma2025interplay}
suggests). Four arms separate them on one Hessian (E2; per-cell values in
the repository): Q$\to$P with the grid re-fitted on the kept
weights, with the mask of pruning alone, or both, and P$\to$Q on a stale grid.
Re-fitting increases the penalty in \resETwoRefitWorse{} of the
\resETwoPenaltyCells{} cells that have one; the pruning-only mask removes at
most \resETwoRemovedPmaskMaxPct\% of it, and a negative share in some cells;
the stale grid changes the error of P$\to$Q by at most
\resETwoStaleMaxAbsPct\%. Neither reading explains the penalty; its cause
remains open.\ifEthree{} In full runs (E3, 50\%+4-bit, SmolLM2-135M), the arms
remove \resEThreeRemovedRefitSmolSmallSFiftyBFourPct\%,
\resEThreeRemovedPmaskSmolSmallSFiftyBFourPct\% and
\resEThreeRemovedBothSmolSmallSFiftyBFourPct\% of the perplexity penalty.\fi


\textbf{Evaluation.} C4 perplexity and KL rank the orders as WikiText-2
perplexity does in \resQTwoAgreePplCFour{} and \resQTwoAgreeKl{} of
\resQTwoAgreeCells{} cells, flips and accuracy in only \resQTwoAgreeFlips{} and
\resQTwoAgreeSrThree.\ifEsix\else{} Flips show what retained accuracy hides:
4-bit SmolLM2-135M keeps \resSrThreeSmolSmallQuantBFour{} of the dense
accuracy (Sr~\cite{laborde2025semantic}, three tasks) but flips
\resFlipsPctSmolSmallQuantBFour\% of its answers.\fi{}
McNemar tests separate P$\to$Q from Q$\to$P in \resMcNemarSigPQQP{} of
\resMcNemarCellsPQQP{} cells ($p<0.05$), not always as perplexity does: at
50\%+4-bit on SmolLM2-135M, P$\to$Q has the lowest perplexity and KL, yet
Q$\to$P answers more items correctly, mostly on SciQ (mean accuracy over the nine tasks,
P$\to$Q minus Q$\to$P: \resAccDiffSmolSmallSFiftyBFourPQQP{} points, 95\% interval
\resAccDiffLoSmolSmallSFiftyBFourPQQP{} to
\resAccDiffHiSmolSmallSFiftyBFourPQQP).

\textbf{Noise.} Within one GPU type results are bit-exact (two L4 machines
give the same likelihood and all \resDetLayers{} layer errors for SmolLM2-135M
at 50\%+4-bit), and fp32 reproduces our fp32 T4 runs of SmolLM2-360M within
\resReproThirtyTwoMaxAbsPct\%.
Across GPU types, the same 16-bit code changes SmolLM2-135M perplexity by
\resHwDeltaSmolSmallQuantBFourPct\% at 4 bits and
\resHwDeltaSmolSmallSFiftyBFourPQPct\% at 50\%+4-bit between a T4 and an L4,
presumably because flipped rounding and mask decisions cascade through the
sequential calibration. Gaps of a few percent between orders are thus weak
evidence on their own.\ifEfive{} Over
three calibration seeds of SmolLM2-360M, the best order is the same in every
seed in \resSeedBestStable{} of \resSeedBestCells{} 4-bit cells, joint beats
P$\to$Q in every seed in \resSeedPairJointPQAllSeeds{} of
\resSeedPairJointPQCells{} (paired $t$-test $p<0.05$ in
\resSeedPairJointPQSig), and the
standard deviation of perplexity reaches \resSeedSdMaxSinglePct\% for single
methods and \resSeedSdMaxCombinedPct\% for combinations.\fi{} With \nmodels{} models
of two families, we claim no scale trend.

\section{Conclusion}

Inside one Hessian-compensated pipeline, quantizing first is the worst order
in most settings, but neither a stale grid nor a mask chosen on rounded
weights explains why, and mask-first orders are sub-additive in aggregate, not
in every layer. Counted honestly, pruning pays only when it replaces a bit of
precision: 50\% pruning with 4-bit weights beats plain 3-bit at 3.0 bits per
weight on \resFrontierThreeWins{} of \resFrontierThreeModels{} models, whereas
25\% pruning with 4-bit weights only ties plain 4-bit at 4.0 bits, and the
zeros that rounding creates count for plain quantization too. We recommend reporting bits per weight with the mask, KL divergence,
flips and paired tests.
